# Strong with Mila — Imagery Guidelines

## 1. Visual identity in one sentence

Strong with Mila visuals should feel calm, feminine, intelligent and grounded: a mix of warm fitness photography, soft editorial wellness, anatomical education, personal notes and quiet strength.

The visual world should feel like:

**calm PT studio + women’s health magazine + biology notebook + warm personal coaching space.**

It should never feel like:

* aggressive gym culture;
* neon fitness branding;
* influencer wellness clichés;
* clinical medical advertising;
* mystical hormone branding;
* overly polished luxury spa content;
* cold corporate health design;
* hypersexualized fitness content.

The goal is to visually communicate:

**Train your body. Fuel your body. Understand your body.**

## 2. Overall visual mood

Images should feel:

* warm;
* soft;
* natural;
* calm;
* grounded;
* feminine but not girly;
* professional but not clinical;
* strong but not aggressive;
* intimate but not overly personal;
* educational but still beautiful.

The viewer should feel:

* safe;
* supported;
* curious;
* capable;
* understood;
* invited into a calm coaching process.

## 3. Colour direction

The visual palette should stay close to:

* soft cream;
* warm beige;
* ivory;
* sand;
* taupe;
* muted clay;
* soft brown;
* dusty rose;
* muted burgundy;
* deep plum;
* soft olive;
* warm grey;
* natural skin tones;
* off-white backgrounds.

Accent colours can include:

* deep plum;
* muted wine;
* terracotta;
* warm brown;
* soft gold;
* olive green.

Avoid:

* neon colours;
* bright pink;
* hot magenta;
* bright purple;
* electric blue;
* saturated orange;
* harsh black-and-white contrast;
* cold grey medical tones;
* artificial colour grading;

Pink can appear subtly as a warm rose or clay tone, but images should not have a strong pink wash.

## 4. Light and photography style

Photography should use:

* natural light;
* soft sunlight;
* warm shadows;
* early morning or late afternoon feeling;
* window light;
* soft contrast;
* gentle highlights;
* creamy backgrounds;
* realistic skin tones;
* minimal editing;
* editorial composition.

Lighting should feel warm and lived-in, not artificial.

Use:

* soft sun patches;
* linen textures;
* warm indoor shadows;
* neutral walls;
* calm Berlin-apartment feeling;
* quiet studio light.

Avoid:

* harsh flash;
* cold studio lighting;
* dark dramatic gym lighting;
* neon gym lighting;
* over-smoothed skin;
* high-gloss influencer retouching;
* fake lens flare;
* unrealistic skin;
* heavy orange filters;
* heavy pink filters.

## 5. Photography composition

Photos should have a lot of breathing room.

Use:

* clean negative space for titles;
* vertical framing for stories and section covers;
* centered or softly off-center subjects;
* natural cropping;
* calm still-life arrangements;
* soft foreground shadows;
* uncluttered backgrounds;
* editorial flat lays;
* partial body crops when appropriate.

Images should be easy to place text over.

For covers, leave empty space in the top third or upper half when possible.

Avoid:

* busy backgrounds;
* too many props;
* cluttered desks;
* crowded gyms;
* overly staged setups;
* extreme close-ups unless intentional;
* awkward cropping of hands, feet or body parts;
* distorted anatomy;
* fake-looking AI hands;
* text generated inside the image unless explicitly requested.

## 8. Illustration style

Illustrations are a major part of the brand.

They should feel like:

* vintage anatomical drawings;
* fine-line medical illustration;
* soft sepia ink;
* women’s health editorial diagrams;
* biology notebook sketches;
* educational but beautiful.

Illustrations should use:

* thin lines;
* delicate hatching;
* warm brown, sepia, muted rose or plum lines;
* cream or transparent backgrounds;
* minimal shading;
* soft anatomical colour;
* clean composition;
* enough detail to feel intelligent, but not so much that it becomes overwhelming.

Preferred line colour:

* dark sepia;
* warm brown;
* muted plum;
* dusty terracotta;
* soft rose-brown.

Avoid:

* black harsh outlines;
* cartoon style;
* children’s illustration;
* glossy 3D anatomy;
* medical textbook gore;
* overly realistic organs;
* neon educational diagrams;
* mystical symbols unless very subtle;
* AI-generated labels inside diagrams unless specifically requested.

## 9. Illustration line thickness

For thin-line illustrations:

* main outline should be fine and elegant;
* line thickness should feel like pen-and-ink anatomy drawing;
* use medium-thin outer contours;
* use very fine internal hatching;
* use delicate dotted lines for signal paths;
* avoid thick icon-like strokes;
* avoid heavy cartoon outlines.

Approximate visual rules:

* outer anatomy lines: thin to medium-thin;
* internal details: very thin;
* hatching: hairline;
* dotted signal lines: fine and light;
* icon lines: slightly thicker than hatching, but still delicate;
* no chunky vector strokes.

If specifying to an AI image generator, use:

“delicate fine-line anatomical illustration, thin sepia ink lines, subtle hatching, soft rose-brown accents, transparent background, editorial women’s health style.”

## 10. Illustration backgrounds

Preferred illustration backgrounds:

* transparent PNG;
* off-white;
* warm cream;
* very light beige.

For reusable assets, transparent background is preferred.

Avoid:

* checkerboard backgrounds pretending to be transparent;
* grey medical backgrounds;
* dark backgrounds;
* busy textures;
* heavy paper textures unless used intentionally.

When generating transparent assets, specify:

“true transparent background PNG, no checkerboard, no paper texture, no coloured background.”

## 12. Image generation prompt rules

When generating AI images, always include:

* vertical image;
* warm natural light;
* soft beige/cream background;
* minimal calm composition;
* editorial wellness photography;
* realistic details;
* no visible text unless requested;
* no distorted hands or faces;
* no extra limbs;
* no logos unless provided and explicitly requested;
* no neon colours;
* no clinical setting unless explicitly needed.

For real photos, use prompt structure:

“Vertical editorial wellness photograph, warm natural window light, soft beige and cream palette, calm minimal studio/apartment setting, realistic woman/women, natural skin texture, neutral activewear, grounded expression, lots of negative space for text, no visible text, no logos, no harsh shadows, no neon colours, realistic proportions.”

For still-life photos, use prompt structure:

“Vertical editorial still-life photograph, warm natural sunlight, soft beige stone surface, linen texture, neutral objects, calm composition, negative space for title, realistic shadows, minimal wellness aesthetic, no visible text, no brand logo unless requested.”

For illustrations, use prompt structure:

“Delicate fine-line anatomical illustration, vintage women’s health editorial style, thin sepia ink lines, subtle rose-brown accents, soft hatching, clean composition, educational but beautiful, true transparent PNG background, no checkerboard, no text labels unless requested.”

## 13. AI image quality control

Before accepting an AI image, check:

* Does it match the colour palette?
* Does it feel calm and intelligent?
* Are hands realistic?
* Are faces realistic?
* Are body proportions natural?
* Are there any strange limbs?
* Is the image too sexualized?
* Is the image too mystical?
* Is the image too clinical?
* Is the image too generic wellness?
* Is the lighting warm and natural?
* Is there enough space for text?
* Is any text misspelled or unwanted?
* Does the image fit Train, Fuel or Understand?

Reject or regenerate if:

* the person has distorted hands;
* the face looks uncanny;
* text is gibberish;
* the image has fake transparency;
* the image feels like stock fitness;
* the image feels like spa/luxury instead of coaching;
* the gym looks too dark or aggressive;
* the cycle imagery feels mystical;
* the nutrition image looks diet-like;
* the anatomy looks medically scary.

## 19. Logo and brand placement

Use the Strong with Mila logo carefully.

Logo can appear on:

* notebook covers;
* intake covers;
* website headers;
* program PDFs;
* branded documents;
* occasional still-life images.

Do not overuse the logo on every image.

When placing logo on generated images:

* use the provided logo as reference whenever possible;
* keep it realistic;
* place it on physical objects like notebooks, forms, or covers;
* keep it in deep plum;
* avoid distorted or misspelled logo text;
* if the logo cannot be replicated cleanly, use no logo instead.

Never allow AI to invent a fake version of the logo with wrong text.

## 20. Text inside AI-generated images

In general, avoid text inside AI-generated images.

Text should usually be added later in Canva, Figma, Adobe Express or another design tool.

AI-generated text often becomes distorted or misspelled.

Exception:

* simple notebook words;
* very short labels;
* logo placement when manually provided;
* handwritten-style words if checked carefully.

If text appears incorrectly, regenerate without text.

## 23. Visual hierarchy by content type

For educational content:

Use diagrams, carousels, simple text, illustrations.

For emotional/trust content:

Use people, conversations, soft portraits, personal moments.

For program/process content:

Use notebooks, tablets, forms, weekly plans, Everfit-style phone screens.

For nutrition content:

Use real food, bowls, meal structure, grocery or meal prep visuals.

For training content:

Use strength movement, equipment, form, focused preparation.

For cycle/body literacy content:

Use notebooks, tracking, anatomical drawings, signal diagrams, calendars.

## 25. Final rule

Strong with Mila imagery should always answer this question:

**Does this image make training, nutrition and body understanding feel calmer, clearer and more personal?**

If yes, it fits.

If it feels loud, fake, shame-based, mystical, clinical or generic, it does not fit.
