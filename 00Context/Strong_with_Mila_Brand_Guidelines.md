## 3. Typography

### Observed typography hierarchy

1. **Primary wordmark: “strong”** — elegant, high-contrast serif, lowercase, pronounced thick/thin strokes.
2. **Secondary wordmark: “with Mila”** — handwritten or brush-style cursive script with personality and a personal touch.
3. **Descriptor: “Women's Strength Coaching”** — clean, modern sans-serif, used in a readable, unobtrusive style.
4. **Board section headers** such as “Core Brand Palette”, “Typography”, “Iconography”, and “Imagery” — large editorial serif in dark plum.
5. **Functional copy and imagery labels** — simpler sans-serif, used for classification, explanations, and guidance.

### Suggested usage guidelines (interpretation; not verified original small print)

- Use the editorial serif for major headings and selected statements.
- Reserve the expressive script for logo/brand moments or very short accents, not long instructional text.
- Use the sans-serif for paragraph copy, guidance, captions, buttons, social post explanations, and accessibility-critical information.
- Keep a clear hierarchy between headline, supporting subtitle, and body copy.
- Ensure text/background contrast, particularly when using dusty coral and pale backgrounds.
- Avoid overly elaborate combinations of multiple decorative display typefaces.

## 5. Imagery guidelines

The source board illustrates six distinct imagery categories. Use these as the primary visual content directions.

### 5.1 Food / Macros without Madness

**Observed examples:** Six overhead or slightly angled food bowls containing balanced combinations of greens, vegetables, grains or other carbohydrates, protein-rich foods, eggs, salmon, and colourful ingredients.

**Visual treatment:**
- Warm, appetising, realistic food photography.
- Simple bowls and plates, restrained styling, natural textures.
- A recognisable mix of vegetables, protein, and carbohydrates.
- Composed, uncluttered overhead food imagery on light neutral surfaces.

**Recommended use:** Meal examples, macro education, practical nutrition explanations, meal-building and sustainable eating content.

### 5.2 Anatomy / Hormones without Hype

**Observed examples:** Fine, muted pink/red scientific-style line drawings, including diagrams of female reproductive anatomy and other body/hormonal systems.

**Visual treatment:**
- Softly coloured, vintage medical/anatomical illustration style.
- Detailed line drawings with plenty of negative space.
- Educational, calm, and non-sensational presentation.

**Recommended use:** Evidence-informed educational material on anatomy, menstrual-cycle physiology, and hormonal systems. Ensure specific claims are checked against reliable sources; illustrations should not be treated as scientific evidence themselves.

### 5.3 Muscles / Strength for Women

**Observed examples:** Six detailed illustrations of muscles and joints, focusing on upper and lower body anatomy, including shoulder, hip, and back musculature.

**Visual treatment:**
- Anatomically inspired realism.
- Warm terracotta/pink muscle areas and soft neutral bone structures.
- Isolated diagrams on clean pale backgrounds.

**Recommended use:** Muscle group explanations, exercise breakdowns, strength mechanics, and training education.

### 5.4 Spine / Posture is not Morality

**Observed examples:** Multiple grey/sepia anatomical images of the spine, pelvis, ribs, and different views of spinal structure and curvature.

**Visual treatment:**
- Fine skeletal drawings and technical anatomical studies.
- Muted, minimally saturated, light-background illustrations.
- Avoid visual dramatization or judgement of natural anatomical differences.

**Recommended use:** Spine anatomy, normal postural variation, movement education, and myth-busting about “good” and “bad” posture. The board's wording explicitly frames posture as **not a moral judgement**.

### 5.5 Systems / Body Literacy

**Observed examples:** Six soft terracotta anatomical diagrams of internal body systems, arranged as a reference series.

**Visual treatment:**
- Detailed medical-style drawings and explanatory anatomical systems.
- Light warm monochromatic colour and clear subject isolation.

**Recommended use:** Accessible explanation of how body systems work, in connection to training, recovery, nutrition, and wellbeing.

### 5.6 Studio / Behind the Method

**Observed examples:** Four photos of a compact strength training studio, with racks, cable or pulley stations, a bench, weights, a clean neutral interior, and minimal decor.

**Visual treatment:**
- Muted neutral lighting, warm beige interiors, dark training equipment.
- Realistic, quiet, unglamorous training environments.
- Documentary/behind-the-scenes rather than loud commercial gym imagery.

**Recommended use:** Coaching process, workout setup, training environment, exercise demonstrations, and the practical work behind the method.

### 5.7 Woman / Behind the Method

**Observed examples:** Six cutout photographs of a woman in muted mauve-grey athletic clothing, in a variety of positions: standing with dumbbells, a portrait from the side, seated, a squat holding a weight, walking, and a back-facing portrait.

**Visual treatment:**
- Natural, athletic, capable, relaxed.
- Neutral/soft clothing colours and clean light backgrounds.
- Mix of movement, stillness, and personal presence.
- Clean cutout photography without a busy environment.

**Recommended use:** Humanising the brand, personal coaching presence, instructional poses, movement demonstrations, and behind-the-method storytelling.

## 7. Practical application guidance

The following are **derived recommendations based on the visible brand board**, not verbatim directions from its unreadable fine print.

### For Instagram and social content

- **Training education:** Use muscle diagrams, subtle plum headers, and line-art dumbbell icons.
- **Nutrition education:** Use realistic food bowls, warm cream backgrounds, and the Fuel icon.
- **Body literacy:** Use medical-style anatomical drawings, minimal text hierarchy, and the Understand icon.
- **Coaching process:** Use neutral studio photographs and images of women training.
- **Carousel design:** Use one major idea per slide, generous margins, warm backgrounds, and brief captions with highly readable sans-serif type.

### For coaching guides and educational PDFs

- Use the cream/off-white colours for long-form reading surfaces.
- Use deep plum for section headings, icons, rules, and select highlights.
- Keep detailed explanations in readable sans-serif typography.
- Use the relevant imagery category instead of generic high-intensity fitness stock photography.
- Label anatomical imagery clearly and distinguish visual metaphor from factual physiology.

### For consistent AI-assisted creative output

When generating design instructions or creative briefs, include the following context:

> Create materials for **Strong with Mila**, a women's strength coaching brand using a warm ivory and muted-earth palette; deep-plum editorial headings; a refined serif plus restrained handwritten brand accent; clear sans-serif body copy; delicate outline icons for **Train, Fuel, Understand**; realistic balanced-food photography; vintage-style anatomical illustrations; and clean, neutral strength-studio or women's training imagery. The aesthetic should feel calm, credible, feminine, grounded, educational, and accessible—not loud, neon, overly glossy, or hyper-commercial.
