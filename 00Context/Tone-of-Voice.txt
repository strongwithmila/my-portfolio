# Strong with Mila — Tone of Voice Guide

## 1. Brand voice in one sentence

Strong with Mila speaks like a calm, knowledgeable coach who understands women’s bodies, explains things clearly, and makes training and nutrition feel supportive instead of overwhelming.

The voice should feel:

* warm;
* intelligent;
* human;
* honest;
* calm;
* practical;
* encouraging;
* evidence-informed;
* non-shaming;
* personal, but still professional.

It should never feel:

* aggressive;
* clinical;
* robotic;
* overly motivational;
* influencer-like;
* fear-based;
* extreme;
* judgmental;
* overly polished to the point of sounding fake.

The feeling should be:

**Biology class + women’s health magazine + calm PT studio + personal notes.**

## 2. How I want people to feel

Every piece of communication should make the client or follower feel:

* understood;
* safe;
* supported;
* informed;
* capable;
* less alone;
* less confused;
* more connected to their body;
* more confident to take action.

The goal is not to impress people with complicated science.

The goal is to help women think:

**“Oh, that makes sense. Maybe my body is not broken. Maybe I just need support, structure and a better understanding of what is happening.”**

## 3. Core message

The core message of Strong with Mila is:

**Train your body. Fuel your body. Understand your body.**

Everything should connect back to this.

**Train** means helping women build strength, confidence, technique and consistency.

**Fuel** means helping women eat in a way that supports energy, training, recovery and real life.

**Understand** means helping women read their body’s signals, notice patterns and make better decisions without shame.

## 4. Brand language principles

### Speak simply, but not superficially

Avoid overcomplicating things. Explain scientific ideas in a way a smart, busy woman can understand without needing a health degree.

Use:
“Your cycle can affect energy, appetite and recovery.”

Instead of:
“Fluctuations in ovarian hormones may modulate substrate metabolism, neuromuscular output and perceived exertion.”

### Be evidence-informed, not science-heavy

Science matters, but the tone should not sound like a textbook. Use practical explanations and avoid making claims stronger than the evidence allows.

Use:
“Some women notice changes in energy or hunger before their period.”

Instead of:
“All women should eat more carbs in the luteal phase.”

### Be warm, not fluffy

The voice should feel supportive, but not vague or overly spiritual.

Use:
“Let’s look at what your body is telling us this week.”

Instead of:
“Your divine feminine wisdom is calling you into alignment.”

### Be direct, not harsh

The tone can be honest and clear, but never punishing.

Use:
“This plan only works if it fits your real life.”

Instead of:
“No excuses. You just need discipline.”

### Be personal, not performative

It can sound like me: human, caring, a little direct, and grounded. It does not need to sound like a perfect wellness brand.

Use:
“Honestly, if a plan needs your life to be perfect, it is not a good plan.”

Instead of:
“Unlock your highest self through optimal lifestyle transformation.”

* no excuses;
* beast mode;
* go hard or go home;
* crush your workout;
* destroy yourself;
* burn it off;
* earn your food;
* sweat is fat crying;

* shred;
* snatched;
* torch calories;
* blast fat;
* punish your body;
* cheat meal;
* clean eating;
* bad foods;
* guilty foods;
* forbidden foods.

Avoid overpromising health language:

* balance your hormones;
* fix your hormones;
* heal your hormones;
* cure PMS;
* cure PCOS;
* reverse endometriosis;
* fix your metabolism;
* reset your metabolism;
* detox your body;
* cleanse;
* inflammation cure;
* fertility boosting;
* guaranteed fat loss;
* transform your body in X days.

Avoid body-shaming language:

* problem areas;
* stubborn fat;
* get rid of;
* tone your flabby arms;
* flatten your belly;
* fix your body;
* bad posture;
* lazy glutes;
* weak core as an insult;
* discipline problem;

Avoid rigid cycle language:

* you must train this way in this phase;
* never lift heavy on your period;
* always do HIIT around ovulation;
* women should not train hard in the luteal phase;
* your hormones control everything;
* cycle syncing rules.

Avoid overly spiritual or mystical language:

* divine feminine;
* womb wisdom;
* moon goddess;
* sacred cycle magic;
* manifest your dream body;
* energetic alignment;
* goddess body;
* feminine frequency.

The brand can be feminine and body-literate without becoming mystical.

## 9. Tone by channel

## Instagram posts

Instagram should be clear, educational, slightly punchy and easy to save.

The tone can be more direct here, but still warm.

Good Instagram tone:

* “Cycle-aware training does not mean changing your entire personality every week.”
* “If you feel hungrier before your period, you are not broken. Your body may simply need more support.”
* “A workout plan that ignores your real life is not a good workout plan.”
* “Macros are not automatically diet culture. It depends how you use them.”

Instagram should use:

* short hooks;
* clear explanations;
* simple examples;
* soft calls to action;
* myth-busting;
* carousels;
* saved-post energy.

Avoid:

* fear-based hooks;
* fake urgency;
* before/after obsession;
* shaming;
* clickbait;
* medical claims.

Good Instagram CTAs:

* “Save this for your next low-energy week.”
* “Send this to a friend who needs to hear it.”
* “Comment STRONG if you want to join the waitlist.”
* “DM me if you want support building a plan that fits your body and your life.”
* “Notice this pattern this week and see what changes.”

## Client messages

Client messages should feel personal, calm and supportive.

They should be clear enough that the client knows exactly what to do next.

Use:

* “Thank you for sharing this.”
* “That makes sense.”
* “Let’s adjust this instead of forcing it.”
* “This is useful information.”
* “This does not mean you failed.”
* “For this week, let’s focus on one thing.”
* “Your next step is simple.”
* “Let’s keep the structure, but reduce the pressure.”

Avoid:

* “You should have…”
* “You just need to…”
* “Try harder.”
* “That is not good.”
* “You fell off.”
* “You failed the plan.”
* “No excuses.”

Example client message:

“Thank you for being honest in your check-in. From what you shared, it looks like sleep and stress made training feel heavier this week, so I do not want us to push harder just for the sake of it. Let’s keep two strength sessions, reduce one accessory set, and focus on protein at breakfast. Small adjustments still count as progress.”

## Forms and intake questions

Forms should feel safe, clear and non-invasive.

Use plain language. Explain why a question is being asked when it may feel personal.

Good form language:

* “Only share what you feel comfortable sharing.”
* “This helps me coach you safely.”
* “This is optional.”
* “This information is confidential.”
* “There is no right or wrong answer.”
* “The goal is to understand your starting point, not judge it.”

Avoid:

* overly medical wording;
* long clinical lists unless needed;
* shame-based food questions;
* asking for unnecessary personal details;
* making everything required.

Good intake description:

“This form helps me understand your goals, training experience, health context, nutrition habits and lifestyle so I can create a safe, personal and realistic coaching plan for you.”

Avoid:

Website CTAs:

## Everfit workout notes

Workout notes should be short, clear and practical.

Each note should tell the client:

* what to focus on;
* how hard it should feel;
* how to adjust;
* what counts as success.

Examples:

“Today is about control and confidence. Keep the effort around RPE 7 and leave 2–3 reps in reserve.”

“Focus on smooth reps, steady breathing and feeling stable through your feet.”

“If energy is low today, reduce the load and keep the movement quality high.”

“Progress is not only adding weight. Better control, better range and more confidence also count.”

Avoid:

* hype language;
* shame;
* pushing intensity at all costs;
* too much explanation inside workout notes.

## Check-in responses

Check-in responses should identify patterns, celebrate effort and give one or two next steps.

Structure:

1. Validate.
2. Identify the pattern.
3. Explain the adjustment.
4. Give the next step.

Example:

“Thank you for the detailed check-in. The main pattern I see is that your hunger increased on the days after lower sleep, which makes sense. This week, let’s keep your training the same but make breakfast more protein-rich and add one planned afternoon snack. We are not changing everything — just giving your body more support.”

## 16. Personal story tone

When talking about my personal story, keep it honest but not overly detailed.

The story should support the method, not become the whole brand.

Use:

* “I know what it feels like to need support and structure.”
* “Motivation alone was not enough for me.”
* “Having guidance changed the way I approached training and food.”
* “That is why I coach with structure, empathy and realistic expectations.”

Avoid:

* over-focusing on weight loss;
* making medication the main story;
* implying my path is the right path for everyone;
* dramatic before/after framing;
* trauma dumping;
* “I fixed myself, so I can fix you.”

## 18. Writing style

Use short paragraphs.

Use clear structure.

Use lists when helpful.

Use plain English.

Avoid long academic paragraphs.

Avoid too many emojis.

Emojis can be used lightly on Instagram, but not excessively.

Preferred emojis, if used:

* 🤎
* ✨
* 📝
* 💪
* 🍽️
* 🌿

Do not overuse:
🔥 😈 🥵 👙 🚨 💣

The tone should feel calm, not loud.

## 19. Brand personality

Strong with Mila is:

* supportive but not soft to the point of vague;
* scientific but not clinical;
* feminine but not mystical;
* structured but not rigid;
* honest but not harsh;
* warm but not overly emotional;
* empowering but not cheesy.

The brand does not shout.

The brand explains, supports and guides.

## 20. Final rule

Whenever writing for Strong with Mila, ask:

1. Does this make women feel more capable?
2. Does this avoid shame?
3. Does this stay within coaching scope?
4. Does this sound like a real human coach?
5. Does this connect to Train, Fuel or Understand?
6. Does this make the next step feel clear?

If yes, it fits the brand.

If it sounds like a fitness influencer, a medical clinic, a wellness guru or a sales funnel, rewrite it.
